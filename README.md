# Magic squares of squares: the rank-one case

This repository is the result of using the concepts found in the paper 
_[Alien Science: Sampling Coherent but Cognitively Unavailable Research Directions from Idea Atoms](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01092v1)_ 
by Alejandro H. Artiles, Martin Weiss, and Levin Brinkmann; however instead of starting with a sample of
machine learning papers, I started with a sample of number theory papers in areas that may be adjacent to
the magic square of squares problem. From that, the following was produced...

Claude Opus 5.5 was used for atomization, ranking and filtering via API.

This repository holds a short paper, a Lean 4 formalization of its main results, and a script that
reproduces its computations.

- [`paper/rank1-exclusion.pdf`](paper/rank1-exclusion.pdf): the paper, 9 pages, with its LaTeX source.
- [`EXPLAINER.md`](EXPLAINER.md): the argument in plain language.
- [`lean/`](lean): the Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization.
- [`scripts/checks.gp`](scripts/checks.gp): a PARI/GP script for the paper's computations.
  [`scripts/checks.out`](scripts/checks.out) is its output. [`scripts/near_misses.gp`](scripts/near_misses.gp)
  checks Remark 5.3, with output [`scripts/near_misses.out`](scripts/near_misses.out).

## The result

Nobody knows whether a 3×3 magic square can have nine distinct perfect squares as entries. Write such a
square in Lucas form, with entries E + αa + βb for α, β ∈ {−1, 0, 1}, and write b = nk². Robertson (1996)
observed that the three progressions E + αa − b, E + αa, E + αa + b give three points P₋₁, P₀, P₁ on the
congruent number curve

  E_n : y² = x³ − n²x,

with x-coordinates (E − a)/k², E/k², (E + a)/k² in arithmetic progression.

**Theorem.** These three points generate a subgroup of E_n(ℚ) of rank at least two. The same holds for the
three points that the a-progressions give.

**Corollary.** In a magic square of squares, the common difference of either diagonal has a squarefree part
n with rank E_n(ℚ) ≥ 2. Such n have density zero (by a theorem of Smith). The smallest are 34, 41, 65, 137,
138 and 145. For example, 1, 25, 49 is never a diagonal.

**Near misses (Remark 5.3).** Two full progressions give two of the three points, but they need not be
independent. On the rank-one curve E₆ there is a magic square with nine distinct entries, six of them
squares, whose two full b-progressions give the points H and 2H. So the theorem really needs all three
progressions. Still, the rank bound alone does not separate magic squares of squares from near misses. In
Bremner's seven-square magic square, and in 2,320 six-square configurations from a computer search, the two
points are independent, so the rank is at least two there too. An exact 2-descent computation proves this,
with no numerical heights. What the paper adds is a proof that every magic square of squares satisfies the
bound.

The key step (Lemma 3.1) works on any elliptic curve with a Weierstrass equation with integer coefficients.
Let Q be a non-torsion point, and suppose every term of its elliptic divisibility sequence from the third
on has a primitive divisor. Then the x-coordinates of three multiples m₁Q, m₂Q, m₃Q with pairwise distinct
|mᵢ| are never in arithmetic progression. On E_n, the primitive divisor theorem of Voutier and Yabuta
(2012), with Verzobio's correction (2021), supplies the hypothesis.

Bremner, Silverman and Tzanakis (2000) proved the corresponding statement for *integral* progressions.
Their theorem never applies to magic squares of squares: in a primitive one, 8 divides a and b, so the
points above never have integral coordinates. The paper proves the rational version that magic squares
need.

This does not settle whether a magic square of squares exists.

## The Lean formalization

[`lean/MagicSquares/RankOne.lean`](lean/MagicSquares/RankOne.lean) proves Lemma 3.1, for every
Weierstrass equation with integer coefficients, as well as Theorem 1.1 and the rank statement of
Corollary 1.2. [`lean/MagicSquares/Basic.lean`](lean/MagicSquares/Basic.lean) defines
`MagicSquareOfSquares` and proves the Lucas form. The headline theorem, for `M : MagicSquareOfSquares`,
is:

```lean
theorem rank_two_of_antidiagonal {n k : ℤ} (hk : k ≠ 0) (hb : M.b = n * k ^ 2)
    (hPD : PrimitiveDivisorTheorem n) (htors : TorsionTheorem n) :
    ∃ P Q : (E n).toAffine.Point, LinearIndependent ℤ ![P, Q]
```

`rank_two_of_diagonal` is the same statement for the main diagonal, with `M.a` in place of `M.b`.
`two_le_finrank_b` and `two_le_finrank_a` state Theorem 1.1 itself, as `Module.finrank ℤ Γ ≥ 2` for the
subgroup Γ generated by the three points.

**Two published theorems enter as explicit hypotheses, not as axioms.** Their full definitions are:

```lean
def HasPrimitiveDivisors {C : WeierstrassCurve ℚ} (Q : C.toAffine.Point) : Prop :=
  ∀ M : ℕ, 3 ≤ M → ∃ ℓ : ℕ, ℓ.Prime ∧ ℓ ∣ (xc (M • Q)).den ∧
    ∀ m : ℕ, 1 ≤ m → m < M → ¬ ℓ ∣ (xc (m • Q)).den

-- Voutier–Yabuta (2012) with Verzobio's correction (2021), for E_n:
def PrimitiveDivisorTheorem (n : ℤ) : Prop :=
  ∀ Q : (E n).toAffine.Point, addOrderOf Q = 0 → IsSquare (xc Q) → HasPrimitiveDivisors Q

-- The torsion of E_n(ℚ) is E_n[2] (classical; Koblitz, Ch. I, §9):
def TorsionTheorem (n : ℤ) : Prop :=
  ∀ P : (E n).toAffine.Point, IsOfFinAddOrder P → 2 • P = 0
```

Here `xc` is the x-coordinate, `W D` is the curve y² = x³ + Dx, and `E n` is `W (-n²)`. Take n to be the
squarefree part of the common difference: then −n² is fourth-power-free and y² = x³ − n²x is a minimal
model, which is the setting of Voutier–Yabuta. Lemma 3.1 (`xc_add_ne_two_mul`) assumes only
`HasIntCoeffs C`, which says that the Weierstrass equation `C` has integer coefficients.

**Everything else is proved in Lean**, including:
- the halving formula, which shows each of the three points is twice a rational point;
- the doubling formula, which shows such points have square x-coordinates;
- the valuation argument of Lemma 3.1;
- the passage from a free group that is not cyclic to rank at least two.

**Axioms.** [`lean/MagicSquares/AxiomCheck.lean`](lean/MagicSquares/AxiomCheck.lean) is part of the build.
It fails unless `#print axioms` reports exactly Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound` for each main theorem. In particular nothing uses `sorry`.

**What a reader still has to check.** Lean guarantees that the theorems follow from the definitions and
hypotheses. It cannot check that the definitions mean what the paper means. Those are the few dozen lines
above, `W`, `E`, `xc` and `HasIntCoeffs` near the top of `RankOne.lean`, and the structure
`MagicSquareOfSquares` in `Basic.lean`.

### Checking the Lean proof

1. Install Lean through [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan).
2. In the `lean` folder, run `lake exe cache get` to download the precompiled Mathlib library.
3. Run `lake build`. It builds the proof and the axiom check; success means everything checked.

The project pins Lean 4.34.0 and Mathlib v4.34.0. The GitHub Actions workflow in
[`.github/workflows/lean.yml`](.github/workflows/lean.yml) runs the same build on every push.

## The computations

Run `gp -q checks.gp > checks.out` in `scripts` with PARI/GP 2.17. It takes under a minute and covers:
- minimality of y² = x³ − n²x for squarefree n ≤ 2000;
- the 2-torsion, 4-torsion and isogenies of E₁, which Smith's density theorem needs;
- the ranks of E_n for squarefree n ≤ 150, and Tunnell's criterion for n = 113;
- the example on E₆, including the table of new primes in the explainer;
- Bremner's seven-square magic square;
- the check of Lemma 3.1 on the rank-one curves with n ≤ 400.

PARI's rank computation searches for points at random. The script fixes the seed, so its output is
reproducible.

`gp -q near_misses.gp > near_misses.out`, also in `scripts`, checks Remark 5.3 in about a second:
- the six-square magic square on E₆ whose two full progressions give dependent points;
- for Bremner's square and the 2,320 six-square configurations in
  [`scripts/near_misses_data.gp`](scripts/near_misses_data.gp), an exact proof that the two points are
  independent. It halves each point and checks that the halves, with the 2-torsion points, have
  independent images under the 2-descent map (Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*,
  Prop. X.1.4).
